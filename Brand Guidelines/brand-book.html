Introduction

Why this brand exists

Have I Been Pwned helps people and organisations understand their exposure to data breaches and take informed action. It combines accessible public services with breach intelligence for the professionals protecting others.

The desired outcome is informed agency: help people understand what is known and what they can do, without fear or reassurance beyond the evidence.

HIBP is founder-led and personally accountable, supported by a visible team. Preserve that human credibility without describing the service as a one-person operation.

Visual references come from existing brand assets and sampled live pages. Editorial standards are formalised in this edition; unvalidated numerical logo rules are not presented as established policy.

Public benefit at the core

Free email checking, notifications and Pwned Passwords coexist with paid professional services.

Transparent

Sources, numbers and methodology are shown, not hidden.

Pragmatic

Matter-of-fact tone over alarmism, even when the news is bad.

Credible

Built on data and evidence, not marketing claims.

One key over. A new word.

A misspelling of "owned" that made itself at home. O and P are neighbours on a QWERTY keyboard. The typo explanation is popular; pinning down one definitive origin is trickier.

YOU GOT PWNED

From bragging rights...

In games, getting pwned means being thoroughly outplayed. The word escaped the lobby: Inverse traces its journey through hacker slang, gaming and wider internet culture. The early origin stories mix history with folklore.

...to useful answers

A different kind of "owned"

In security, the word points to being controlled or compromised. With HIBP, the question is whether your information appeared in a data breach. It is not a scorecard, a judgement or proof that someone controls your device.

A cheeky name. A serious job.

We borrow the internet's vocabulary, not its trash talk. No gaming headset required, and no blame for being caught in a breach.

Logo System

The symbol

The icon is a stylised ';--: an apostrophe, a semicolon and two hyphens. This pattern represents a component of a SQL injection attack, the vector by which many data breaches in HIBP were caused.

The tall left bar represents the apostrophe. The stacked pair forms the semicolon's dot and lower tail. The two short bars on the right are the hyphens: four characters become five parallelograms.

Use the supplied artwork, not typed punctuation. The shapes interpret the characters rather than reproducing a typeface.

  • Preserve the supplied forward-leaning angle
  • Works as a single-colour mark in white, black or Pwned Blue
  • Use the standalone asset where a full wordmark is impractical; check legibility at the final size

Logo System

Precise geometry, deliberate alignment

Five parallelograms share a forward lean, equal horizontal widths and carefully aligned edges.

Five parallelograms

The ';-- pattern becomes one apostrophe bar, two semicolon bars and two hyphen bars. All five have parallel opposite sides and the same horizontal width w.

Edges align across the gaps

The stacked pair follows common slanted side lines. The dashes share top and bottom edges; all four upper pieces finish on one horizontal baseline.

Preserve the construction

Scale the supplied icon as one unit. Never adjust individual bars, gaps or angles. Guides explain the design; rounded SVG coordinates are not a redrawing specification.

Logo System

Three forms, one identity

Choose the form for the available space, audience familiarity and how clearly the brand needs to introduce itself.

Icon only

The most compact expression. Use for avatars, favicons, loading states and small placements where HIBP is already identified.

On merchandise, it works as a discreet chest detail or a recognisable cap or visor emblem. It does not spell out the brand for a new audience.

Icon + Pwned

A compact, readable wordmark for navigation, horizontal spaces and audiences who recognise the name.

Use on tees, polos and caps, from small chest embroidery to a larger central statement. Keep the icon and lettering together as supplied.

Icon + Have I Been Pwned

The full introduction. Use for covers, prominent brand introductions and audiences who may not know what the icon or "Pwned" refers to.

The stacked form suits drinkware and taller spaces. It also appears as a small chest mark on the store's Classic Hoodie when the full name remains legible.

All three examples are complete SVGs from the repository's Logos folders, shown in their original black treatment. The website's gradient hero is a colour treatment of the full form, not a fourth form.

Inspired by the API demo title: the pattern extends to longer wording. The layout accommodates the words without stretching the icon or condensing the lettering.

Carry the family resemblance

Pair the supplied icon with bold italic Switzer lettering, balancing scale, alignment and spacing. Use a coherent pale-blue-to-Pwned-Blue gradient on a dark background. Size or reflow long titles; never distort them.

Keep identity and content distinct

Reserve this treatment for destination, feature and editorial titles, not every label. Keep HIBP attribution clear and existing primary logos intact. Title artwork does not rename the organisation or add a fourth primary form.

Voice & Tone

How HIBP sounds

We are

  • Direct and matter-of-fact
  • Pragmatic, evidence-based and credible
  • Quietly witty, never forced
  • Transparent about what data is and isn't shown
  • Human and commercially clear without pressure

We are not

  • Alarmist or fear-mongering
  • Bureaucratic or needlessly jargon-heavy
  • Preachy about security hygiene
  • Salesy or hype-driven
  • Vague about sources or methodology
Use precisely

Explain "pwned" when needed. Use "exposed" for a breach finding, "compromised" when supported, and "free" for the specific service that is free.

Avoid assumptions

Do not equate every finding with being "hacked". Prefer "affected person" outside legal contexts. Avoid unsupported superlatives and "guaranteed safe".

Understated humour belongs in the brand, not at an affected person's expense. Be direct about risk without blame, ridicule or pressure to buy.

Positioning

One brand, different needs

Accessible public services and professional breach intelligence share one promise: help people understand exposure and take informed action.

For individuals

Lead with the task. Explain the finding in plain language and give proportionate next steps.

Clearly identify free email checking, notifications and Pwned Passwords. Do not imply every HIBP service is free.

Example

Check if your email address is in a data breach.

For professionals

Lead with coverage, integration, privacy controls and operational limits. Be precise, not bureaucratic.

Explain paid capabilities and plan boundaries without using breach findings to pressure a purchase.

Example

Monitor domain exposure and integrate breach intelligence into your workflows.

For media & partners

Lead with attributable evidence, dated statistics and the exact scope of the relationship.

Show the people behind the service without repeating the outdated claim that one person runs everything.

Example

Independently owned and operated, with public benefit at the core.

Name consistently. Use Have I Been Pwned on first mention and HIBP thereafter. Retain Pwned Passwords as the service name. The compact logo is artwork, not a replacement spelling of the organisation's name.

Communication

Explain exposure, not certainty

No match found

"This email address wasn't found in the breaches loaded into HIBP. That does not rule out exposure elsewhere."

Do not say "Your account is safe" or imply complete coverage.

Exposure found

"Your email address appeared in this breach. Review the affected data and the recommended next steps."

A historical finding does not automatically establish current account takeover.

New notification

Separate the breach date from the date added to HIBP. Newly discovered or newly loaded data may be years old.

State affected data classes and relevant next steps. Where passwords were exposed, advise changing affected or reused passwords.

Privacy reassurance

Describe the safeguard for the specific feature. Pwned Passwords and email searches have different data flows.

Do not claim every search is anonymous or nothing is ever logged. Use wording consistent with the current privacy policy and product documentation.

Respect the person. Avoid blame and jokes about exposure. Use synthetic examples in demonstrations, and never expose a person's sensitive breach history in brand materials.

Trust

Show why people can trust HIBP

Be specific about relationships

Distinguish recommendations, integrations, data contributions and customer relationships. None automatically implies certification or blanket endorsement.

Prefer a sourced statement such as "Referenced in NCSC guidance" to an unsupported claim of government approval.

Make numbers accountable

Label what is counted, cite the source and include an as-of date in published materials. Addresses and records are not the same as unique people.

Refresh statistics before publication. Distinguish verified findings, allegations and uncertainty.

Separate sponsorship from findings

Label sponsored content where it appears and keep it visually distinct from breach results. Explain commercial benefits without fear-based selling.

A partner's protection claim is not an HIBP capability. Public benefit does not mean HIBP is a charity or that every service is free.

Keep the human accountability

Describe HIBP as independently owned and operated, founder-led and supported by a team. Explain methodology rather than relying on personality alone.

Use the current About page for team descriptions. Avoid "one person running everything".
